# Style

## Samples

The bus left her at the crossroads with the engine still running, as if the driver did not want to stop long enough to be asked anything. Ilse stood in the dust and let it go. There was a sign for the village, half its letters gone, and a dog asleep under it.

She had not told anyone she was coming. That had seemed like a decision on the train. Now it seemed like the kind of thing her sister would say about her later.

The road went down between two walls. Somebody had stacked the stones without mortar a long time ago and somebody else had kept them up. She touched one as she passed. It was warm.

At the bottom of the hill a woman was hanging sheets. She looked at Ilse's suitcase and then at Ilse.

"You're the Brandt girl," she said.

"I was."

The woman pegged a corner and did not ask what that meant.

## Description

Close third person, past tense, one point of view per scene. Short declarative sentences, with the occasional longer one that runs on with "and". Plain words. Feelings are shown through what people do or decline to do, and are rarely named. Dialogue is sparse and people rarely say exactly what they mean. Paragraphs are short. No similes stacked on similes; one concrete detail is better than three.
